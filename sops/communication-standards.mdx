Consistent, professional communication is one of the top drivers of client satisfaction at Hellenic Technologies. Every team member who interfaces with clients must follow these standards. Poor communication — delayed responses, unclear emails, missed meeting notes — erodes client trust far faster than technical issues do. These standards exist to protect both the client relationship and the agency's reputation.

---

## Response Time Standards

Acknowledge every client message within the timeframes below. If you cannot provide a full answer within the window, send an acknowledgement confirming that you have received the message and when you will follow up.

| Channel | Response Time |
|---|---|
| Email | Within 4 business hours |
| WhatsApp / Messaging | Within 2 business hours (if used) |
| Phone | Return missed calls within 2 hours |
| Project management tool comments | Within 4 business hours |

Business hours are Monday–Friday, 09:00–18:00 EET (Athens time). For clients in other time zones, agree a communication window upfront and document it in the project record.

## Email Etiquette

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use the Official Email Signature">
    Always use the HT email signature template. The template is available in the shared drive. Do not create your own version or omit the signature on client emails.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write Clear Subject Lines">
    Format subject lines as: `[ProjectName] — [Topic]`. For example: `[Starbucks GR] — Weekly Status Update` or `[Omron EU] — Revised Proposal v2`. This makes emails easy to search and thread correctly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="CC the Account Manager">
    Copy the Account Manager on all client communications unless you have an explicit, documented agreement to do otherwise. This keeps the AM informed and prevents clients from receiving conflicting messages.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Maintain Professional Tone">
    No informal language, emojis, or overly casual phrasing in client emails. Match the client's register — if they are formal, stay formal. If they are friendly and casual, you can be warm but still professional.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send Meeting Notes Promptly">
    Attach or link meeting notes within 24 hours of any client call or meeting. Notes should include a summary of discussion points, decisions made, and a clear list of action items with owners and deadlines.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Meeting Standards

Every client meeting — whether a kick-off call, a weekly check-in, or an ad hoc discussion — must follow this protocol.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Send Agenda in Advance" icon="list-check">
    Share an agenda at least 24 hours before any meeting. Even a brief bullet-point list helps the client prepare and keeps the call focused.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Be On Time" icon="clock">
    Join calls on time. If you are running late for any reason, notify the client immediately via message — do not simply arrive late without warning.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Take and Share Notes" icon="pen-to-square">
    Designate one person to take notes during every call. Share the notes to all attendees within 24 hours, including the client.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Confirm Next Steps" icon="arrow-right">
    End every meeting by confirming action items, owners, and deadlines. Repeat these verbally before closing the call and include them at the top of the meeting notes.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Reporting Cadence

Clients should never need to ask for an update — updates should arrive before they're needed. Follow these default cadences unless the contract specifies otherwise.

| Client Type | Report Frequency |
|---|---|
| Active project clients | Weekly status updates |
| Retainer clients | Monthly performance reports |
| Campaign clients | Weekly or bi-weekly (per contract) |

Reports should be concise and results-focused. Lead with performance highlights, then flag any issues or risks, and close with the plan for the next period.

## Escalation Triggers

Certain situations require senior team or management involvement. Do not try to manage the following on your own — escalate immediately:

- Client complaint about quality or delivery timelines
- Budget or scope dispute
- Potential or threatened contract termination
- Any legal or compliance concern (GDPR, IP, contractual obligation)

When escalating, brief the senior team member fully before they contact the client. Never put a senior colleague in a situation where they are surprised by information the client already knows.

<Warning>
Never escalate a conflict in writing. If a situation is tense or adversarial, pick up the phone. Written messages are easily misread, tend to harden positions, and create a paper trail that can complicate resolution. A direct conversation almost always moves faster toward a solution.
</Warning>
