Hellenic Technologies is certified to ISO/IEC 27001 (Information Security Management) and ISO/IEC 27701 (Privacy Information Management). Every team member is responsible for upholding these security standards in their daily work — not just the IT or DevOps teams. A single security lapse can expose client data, create legal liability, and permanently damage client relationships. These practices are non-negotiable.

---

## Password and Access Management

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use the Company Password Manager">
    Store all work credentials exclusively in the company password manager. Never use personal password managers, browser-saved passwords, sticky notes, spreadsheets, or plain text documents. If you are not yet set up on the company password manager, contact IT immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Strong Passwords">
    All passwords must be a minimum of **16 characters** and include a mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols. Use the password manager's built-in generator — do not create passwords manually.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enable MFA on All Work Accounts">
    Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) is mandatory on all work accounts, including: Google Workspace, AWS, Cloudflare, Slack, project management tools, and any client platform you access. If a platform supports MFA and you have not enabled it, do so now.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Share Credentials Safely">
    Never share passwords via email, Slack, WhatsApp, or any other messaging channel. Use the secure sharing feature within the company password manager. If a colleague needs access, add them to the relevant vault entry — do not copy and paste credentials into a message.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Client Credential Handling

Client credentials are among the most sensitive assets we manage. A breach of client credentials can result in ad account hijacking, data exposure, financial damage, and contract termination.

- Store all client credentials in the **designated secure folder** within the company password manager. Every client should have their own named folder or vault.
- **Never** store client passwords in plain text documents, spreadsheets, Notion pages, Google Docs, or chat messages — even temporarily.
- Use **separate credentials** for each client. Never use Hellenic Technologies master credentials to access a client's platform on their behalf — request dedicated access credentials for the agency.
- When a team member leaves the company or changes role, **revoke their access immediately** from all platforms — both HT internal tools and any client platforms they had access to. This is the responsibility of the account manager and team lead jointly. Do not wait for an offboarding checklist to prompt this.

## Device Security

All devices used for work — whether company-issued or personal (BYOD) — must meet the following requirements:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Full-Disk Encryption" icon="lock">
    Enable full-disk encryption on all work devices. On macOS, use FileVault. On Windows, use BitLocker. Contact IT if you need help enabling this.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Lock Screen Required" icon="display">
    Set a PIN or password lock on your work device with a screen timeout of no more than **5 minutes**. Enable auto-lock when the device is unattended.
  </Card>
  <Card title="No Client Data on Personal Devices" icon="mobile">
    Do not store, download, or sync client data to personal devices without explicit written approval from your team lead and the DPO. This includes documents, analytics exports, and ad account data.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use VPN on Public Networks" icon="shield">
    Connect to the company VPN whenever you are accessing client systems, internal tools, or cloud infrastructure from a public or untrusted network (cafés, airports, hotels, co-working spaces).
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Access Control Principles

Follow the principle of **least privilege**: every team member and system should have access only to what they need to do their job — nothing more.

- Request access to new platforms or environments through your team lead. Do not self-provision access to client accounts or internal systems beyond your current role.
- Review your active platform accesses quarterly and remove any that are no longer needed.
- Shared accounts are not permitted on critical systems. Every individual must have their own named account where the platform supports it.

## Incident Response

If you discover or suspect a security issue — including a potential breach, unauthorised access, lost or stolen device, or suspicious account activity — take the following steps immediately:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Stop and Report">
    Do not attempt to investigate, remediate, or cover up the issue yourself. Report to **security@hellenictechnologies.com** immediately and contact your team lead by phone.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Preserve Evidence">
    Do not delete emails, logs, or files related to the incident. Preserve everything as-is until the security team instructs otherwise.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow Security Team Instructions">
    The security team will lead the response. Follow their instructions precisely. Do not communicate about the incident externally (including to the client) until authorised to do so.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document Your Actions">
    Write down what you observed, when you noticed it, and what actions you have taken — including exact timestamps. This log will be essential for the incident post-mortem and any regulatory reporting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
Phishing attacks are the most common entry point for security breaches. If you receive a suspicious email — even one that appears to come from a colleague, a client, or a trusted platform — do **not** click any links, open any attachments, or enter any credentials. Report it to IT immediately by forwarding the email to security@hellenictechnologies.com. When in doubt, verify by calling the sender directly.
</Warning>
