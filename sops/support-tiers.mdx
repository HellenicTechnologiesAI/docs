All client support requests at Hellenic Technologies are classified by priority tier. Correct classification ensures the right team responds within the right timeframe and keeps SLA commitments on track. When you receive a support request — whether by email, phone, or via the project management tool — your first job is to classify it correctly before assigning it or beginning work.

---

## Priority Tiers

| Priority | Definition | Examples | Response Target | Resolution Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 – Critical** | Service completely down or major business impact | Website offline, ad campaigns suspended, confirmed data breach | 1 hour | 4 hours |
| **P2 – High** | Major feature broken, significant business impact | Contact forms not working, tracking broken across a site, campaign delivery errors | 2 hours | 8 hours |
| **P3 – Medium** | Non-critical issue, workaround available | Content errors, minor display issues, report generation delay | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| **P4 – Low** | Minor request, enhancement, or general question | Copy tweaks, design feedback, general how-to questions | 8 hours | 72 hours |

**Response Target** is the time from when the request is received to when the client receives an initial acknowledgement and a named owner for the issue.

**Resolution Target** is the time from receipt to full resolution or a documented workaround being in place.

## How to Classify a Request

Assess **business impact first**. Ask yourself: is this stopping the client from operating, losing them money, or exposing them to risk right now?

<Steps>
  <Step title="Determine Business Impact">
    Is the client's website, campaign, or core service completely non-functional? → **P1**. Is a major feature broken with no workaround, causing active revenue loss? → **P2**. Is there a problem but the client can still operate? → **P3** or **P4**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consider Visibility">
    Issues visible to the client's end users (broken pages, failed forms, missing campaigns) generally warrant a higher priority than back-end or cosmetic issues.
  </Step>
  <Step title="When in Doubt, Escalate the Classification">
    If you are unsure whether a request is P2 or P3, err on the side of the higher priority and check with your team lead. It is better to over-respond than to miss an SLA on a genuinely critical issue.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document the Classification">
    Log the priority level in the CRM or project management tool when you create the support ticket. Include your reasoning if the classification is not obvious.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## After Receiving a Request

Regardless of priority, every support request must be acknowledged within the response window. A good acknowledgement includes:

- Confirmation that the request has been received
- The assigned priority tier
- The name of the person handling it
- An estimated next-update time (for P1/P2: within 1 hour; for P3/P4: within the response window)

Never leave a client in silence — even if you do not yet have an answer, communicate proactively.

<Note>
Clients on formal SLA or BAU retainer contracts have **contractually defined** response and resolution times that may differ from the standard targets above. Always check the client's contract in the CRM before committing to a timeline. If you cannot locate the contract, ask the Account Manager before responding to the client.
</Note>
