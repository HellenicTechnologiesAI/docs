SLA (Service Level Agreement) and BAU (Business As Usual) retainers are premium support offerings that commit Hellenic Technologies to defined response and resolution times. Account Managers must understand these commitments fully and ensure the team can deliver against them before any SLA contract is signed. Overpromising on SLA terms — or signing a contract without team buy-in — creates legal exposure and damages client trust.

---

## What an SLA Retainer Includes

SLA retainers go beyond standard support. Clients on an SLA retainer can expect:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Contractual Response Times" icon="timer">
    Defined P1–P4 response and resolution targets, documented in the contract and legally binding. These override the standard support tier defaults.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Dedicated Point of Contact" icon="user-tie">
    A named Account Manager and technical contact who the client can reach directly for escalations.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Monthly SLA Performance Report" icon="chart-bar">
    A report delivered each month showing ticket volumes, response times, resolution times, and SLA compliance percentage versus target.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Regular Service Review Meetings" icon="calendar-check">
    Scheduled (typically quarterly) service reviews where performance is discussed, priorities are aligned, and any contract adjustments are agreed.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Standard SLA Tiers

The table below shows the default SLA targets used in standard contracts. The actual targets for any given client are defined in their specific contract — always check the contract before committing to a timeline.

| Priority | Response Target | Resolution Target |
|---|---|---|
| P1 – Critical | 1 hour | 4 hours |
| P2 – High | 2 hours | 8 hours |
| P3 – Medium | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| P4 – Low | 8 hours | 72 hours |

Some enterprise clients negotiate tighter targets, particularly on P1. Make sure the operational team is aware of and has agreed to any custom SLA terms before they are committed in a contract.

## Measuring SLA Compliance

Accurate measurement is essential — both for client reporting and for internal quality management.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Log All Tickets With Timestamps">
    Every support request must be logged in the CRM or support tool immediately upon receipt with a precise timestamp. This is when the SLA clock starts — not when the ticket is first reviewed, assigned, or acknowledged.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign Priority at Intake">
    Classify the ticket with the correct priority tier at the point of logging. Reclassifying a ticket later can distort SLA measurements. If you are unsure, assign a provisional priority and escalate for confirmation within 30 minutes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record All Status Transitions">
    Log timestamps for each status change: received → acknowledged → in progress → resolved. These transitions are the data behind the SLA report.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate Monthly SLA Report">
    At the end of each calendar month, generate the SLA compliance report from the support tool. Review it before sending to the client — flag any breaches proactively rather than letting the client discover them in the report.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## SLA Breach Protocol

An SLA breach occurs when a response or resolution target is missed. Handle breaches proactively — waiting for the client to raise it makes the situation significantly harder to manage.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Acknowledge the Breach Proactively">
    As soon as you identify that an SLA target has been or will be missed, notify the client immediately. Do not wait for them to notice. A proactive acknowledgement demonstrates ownership and preserves trust.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apologise and Explain">
    Provide a clear, honest explanation of what caused the breach. Do not offer vague non-apologies. Clients respect honesty significantly more than corporate deflection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provide a Remediation Plan">
    Tell the client exactly what steps are being taken to resolve the issue and prevent a recurrence. Give a concrete timeline for resolution if one is available.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log Internally as an Incident">
    Every SLA breach must be logged as an internal incident in the CRM. This creates the record needed for the post-mortem and allows trends to be identified over time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess Service Credit Obligations">
    Review the client's contract for any service credit provisions tied to SLA breaches. If a credit applies, flag it to Finance and the Account Manager so it can be processed correctly and promptly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## BAU Retainers

BAU (Business As Usual) retainers cover ongoing, predictable work that falls outside active project scope — for example, regular content updates, monitoring, managed analytics, or managed digital marketing. BAU retainers are managed like a retainer hours arrangement, with the addition of SLA response commitments where specified in the contract.

Key differences from project work:

- BAU work is typically scoped in hours per month, not deliverables
- Unused BAU hours do not always roll over — check the contract
- BAU scope changes must be agreed in writing before work begins
- Monthly utilisation reports should be sent to the client alongside the SLA performance report

<Warning>
Never agree to SLA terms in a proposal, email, or call without first consulting the relevant team leads to confirm the team can meet those commitments. SLA contracts are legally binding. If an SLA is breached and the client invokes the contract, HT may be liable for service credits or, in serious cases, contract termination. All SLA commitments must be signed off by the Account Manager, team lead, and a member of the management team before the contract is executed.
</Warning>
