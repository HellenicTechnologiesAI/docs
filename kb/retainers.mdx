Retainers are the backbone of Hellenic Technologies' recurring revenue and long-term client relationships. This guide explains how to set up, manage, and report on retainer engagements — including hour bank retainers, SLA retainers, and managed service retainers.

## Retainer Types

There are three core retainer structures at HT:

- **Retainer Hours** — the client buys a monthly bank of hours (e.g., 10h, 20h, 40h) usable across any HT service. Flexible and cross-functional.
- **SLA/BAU Retainer** — a formal Service Level Agreement with defined response and resolution times for ongoing business-as-usual support. Suited to clients with mission-critical systems.
- **Managed Service Retainers** — service-specific ongoing engagements (Managed Servers, Managed Marketing, Managed BI, etc.). Detailed on the [After Sales](/kb/after-sales) page.

## Retainer Hours vs SLA Retainer

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Retainer Hours">
    ### How It Works

    The client purchases a fixed monthly bank of hours that can be used across any Hellenic Technologies service — development, design, analytics, marketing, BI, or any combination.

    ### Key Features

    - **Flexible**: hours can be split across services within the same month
    - **Tracked monthly**: all time logged against the client in the time-tracking system with task-level detail
    - **Rollover policy**: unused hours may roll over to the following month — check the individual contract for rollover terms (not all contracts include rollover)
    - **Monthly report**: send the client a report at month end showing hours used, tasks completed, and hours remaining

    ### Common Hour Banks

    | Tier | Hours/Month | Best For |
    |---|---|---|
    | Starter | 10h | Small clients, light ongoing needs |
    | Standard | 20h | Mid-size clients, regular updates across 1–2 services |
    | Growth | 40h | Active clients across multiple service lines |
    | Enterprise | 80h+ | Complex ongoing engagements; custom pricing |

    ### Billing

    Billed monthly in advance. Overages (hours used beyond the contracted bank) are billed at the standard hourly rate at month end. Always notify the client before exceeding their hour bank — never run up overages without prior approval.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="SLA / BAU Retainer">
    ### How It Works

    A formal SLA retainer defines the scope of BAU (Business As Usual) support and sets contractual response and resolution times for incidents and requests. Designed for clients who require guaranteed response times and accountability.

    ### SLA Priority Tiers

    | Priority | Description | Response Time | Resolution Target |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **P1 — Critical** | System down, complete service failure | 2 hours | 4 hours |
    | **P2 — High** | Major functionality impaired, significant business impact | 4 hours | 8 hours |
    | **P3 — Medium** | Minor issues, non-critical functionality affected | 8 hours | 24 hours |
    | **P4 — Low** | General requests, BAU changes, non-urgent tasks | 1 business day | Per agreed schedule |

    Response times are measured during business hours (09:00–18:00 EET, Mon–Fri) unless the contract specifies 24/7 coverage.

    ### Incident Management

    - All incidents and requests must be logged via the agreed channel (email, ticketing system, or Slack — per the contract)
    - Assign each ticket a priority level at intake
    - Acknowledge the ticket within the SLA response window
    - Provide regular updates until resolved — do not go silent on open incidents
    - Close tickets with a summary of what was done and any preventive steps taken

    ### Monthly SLA Report

    Send the client a monthly SLA report covering:
    - Total tickets raised by priority level
    - SLA compliance rate (target: 100% on P1/P2)
    - Any SLA breaches — explain and document the root cause
    - Upcoming maintenance or planned changes

    ### Billing

    Billed monthly in advance at the contracted SLA retainer fee. Any work outside the defined BAU scope is billed separately via a change request or retainer hours.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Billing Overview

| Retainer Type | Billing Cycle | Overage Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Retainer Hours | Monthly, in advance | Billed at standard hourly rate at month end |
| SLA/BAU Retainer | Monthly, in advance | Out-of-scope work via separate change request |
| Managed Service | Monthly, in advance | Scope changes via contract amendment |

All retainer invoices are raised by the Account Manager and sent to the client's billing contact on the agreed invoice date. Ensure the CRM reflects the correct billing contact and invoice schedule.

## Monthly Retainer Management Checklist

Run through this checklist at the end of each month for every retainer client:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Log All Hours">
    Ensure every team member who worked on the account has logged their time accurately in the time-tracking system. Review for completeness before running the monthly report.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compile Monthly Report">
    Prepare the monthly client report:

    - **Retainer Hours clients**: hours used vs. contracted, breakdown by task/service, hours remaining, summary of work completed
    - **SLA clients**: ticket volume by priority, SLA compliance rate, any breaches with root cause, planned work for next month
    - **Managed service clients**: service-specific performance summary (e.g., analytics report, campaign performance, server uptime)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send Report to Client">
    Send the report to the client's agreed contact by the agreed date (typically the first week of the following month). Use the standard report template for the relevant service type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Flag Approaching Hour Limits">
    If the client has used or is projected to use **80% or more** of their monthly hours by mid-month, notify the Account Manager immediately. The AM should contact the client to discuss priorities for the remaining hours or authorise an overage if needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Annual Retainer Review">
    Once per year (typically at contract renewal), review the retainer scope:

    - Has the client consistently had overages? Recommend upgrading to a larger hour bank.
    - Has the client consistently had unused hours? Discuss whether the retainer is right-sized.
    - Are there new services the client could benefit from? Identify upsell opportunities.
    - Update the contract and CRM if the scope changes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Never let a client's retainer hours run out without notifying them first. Running hours down to zero — especially without the client's knowledge — creates billing disputes, damages trust, and can leave critical work unfinished. Always flag at 80% usage. If you're close to the limit and the client has urgent work, escalate to the Account Manager immediately to get an overage approved before proceeding.
</Warning>
